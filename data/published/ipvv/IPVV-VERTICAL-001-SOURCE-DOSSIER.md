## 1. THE VERTICAL-1 TARGET — one serious IPVV debate

**VERTICAL-1 = the Pratyabhijñā (recognition) argument that the Buddhist "determination"
(adhyavasāya) cannot establish an external object.**

Why this cluster (per the directive: clear thesis · multiple premises · opponent · reply ·
qualification · real crux · secondary scholarship available):
- **clear thesis**: the cognition never establishes a thing outside itself (there is nothing outside
  the self-luminous awareness for it to reach).
- **multiple premises**: (a) the determination is error-form; (b) the inert part cannot establish;
  (c) the non-inert part is the pure self-experience, un-divided from the memory-cognition, hence not
  external-natured.
- **opponent**: the Buddhist (the yogācāra/Dignāga-line determination theorist), who holds the
  cognition takes an external object via the determination.
- **reply**: the Śaiva reply to the Buddhist's "let it be both erroneous and establishing" and to the
  "as fire burns the wood though inert" objection.
- **qualification**: the appearance is drawn-to-the-external "only for the slow-minded, in the
  illusion and the diseased-eye case" — not universally.
- **real crux**: what licenses the establishing power — the self-luminous awareness (prakāśa) itself,
  not any inert reaching. This is the recognition doctrine's core (cit → prakāśa/vimarśa).
- **secondary scholarship**: Ratié, Torella's IPK/IPVV work, the recognition literature on
  adhyavasāya and the external-object problem.

## 3. THE TEXT (L2, frozen reference) — key spans

The full frozen l2_text is the source of truth for VERTICAL-1. The load-bearing spans:

```
(a) "The determination cannot reach outside."
    The Buddhist falls back on the determination (adhyavasāya) — the act by which, he thinks, the
    cognition is taken to establish an external thing. Abhinavagupta cuts this last thread.

(b) "From the very first, the appearance shining in the consciousness and in the determination is
    taken as external — but only for the slow-minded, in the illusion and the diseased-eye case."

(c) "He who knows the cause of the appearance does not determine its externality at all."

(d) "The external, which the error-form determination was supposed to establish, has no standing in
    that very part: for the part is inert, and an inert thing cannot establish."

(e) "In the part that is not inert — the non-construction, the pure self-experience — there the
    establishment is not made either, for that part shines only its own form and its own
    self-appearance."

(f) "The non-erroneous part, the self-experience, is un-divided from the memory-cognition — it is not
    a separate thing reaching out to an external — and so it is not external-natured."

(g) "The establishing is in the self; the experience, with the manifestation at its head, is the
    establishing, and it is self-luminous, not a thing that reaches a thing."

(h) "The self is one, self-luminous, and all — the memory, the determination, the experience — are its
    own shining."
```

Sanskrit: the M00020 volume locates the adhyavasāya discussion in the jñānādhikāra of the
vivṛtivimarśinī (the passage's `c1` cites the *dṛḍhābhyāsavāsanā* "firm practice-impression").

## 4. THE RECONSTRUCTION REFERENCE (what the machinery should produce)

Author (Abhinavagupta) — Position A:
```
THESIS  C1: The cognition never establishes a thing outside itself.
P1   The determination (adhyavasāya) is error-form (it is the drawing of the appearance outward).
P2   An inert part cannot establish (the inert = dead part of the determination cannot establish an external).
P3   The non-inert part (pure self-experience, vimarśa) is un-divided from the memory-cognition and is
     not external-natured.
P4   (implicit warrant) Establishing power belongs only to self-luminous awareness (prakāśa), not to a
     thing that reaches a thing.
=>  C1 (therefore the determination establishes nothing external; the external is only drawn-to by the
    impression, never established).

Opponent (Buddhist) — Position B:
O1   The determination establishes an external object (that is its role).
O2   Reply to "inert can't establish": the determination may be BOTH erroneous and establishing.
O3   (analogy) As fire burns wood though inert, so the determination establishes the object; why ask
     whether it is inert or conscious?

Śaiva reply — Position A:
R1   "let it be both erroneous and establishing — what is the harm?" → fails: the reason is inertness;
     an inert thing cannot establish.
R2   "as fire burns the wood though inert" → the establishing is in the self; experience (with the
     manifestation at its head) IS the establishing, and it is self-luminous, not a thing that reaches
     a thing.

QUALIFICATION  Q: the outward-drawing is only for the slow-minded (the illusion and diseased-eye case);
     he who knows the cause does not determine externality at all.
```

## 5. THE CRUX (the smallest dispute that changes the conclusion)

> Which assumption about the establishing power must hold for the recognition inference to succeed?
> Specifically: does "establishing" require the self-luminous awareness (prakāśa) itself — such that
> the inert part cannot establish and the external is only drawn-to — or can a non-self-luminous (inert)
> representation nonetheless establish an external, as the Buddhist's fire-burning-wood analogy asserts?

The decisive premise is **P2 (inertness blocks establishing) + P4 (establishing = self-luminous)**.
Remove P2 → the Buddhist's O3 analogy succeeds and the external might be established; accept O3 →
the conclusion C1 fails. This is the load-bearing crux: **CRUX-IPVV-001 — the self-luminosity of the
establishing act.**
